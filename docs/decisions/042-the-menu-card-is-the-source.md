# 042 — The menu card is the source; there is no paper form

27 September. The owner: _"I did not fill up the paper forms — this is time consuming and also
makes no sense. If Jain food is available, it goes into the menu card, and it's ridiculous to ask a
non-veg restaurant if they'd serve Jain food."_

This replaces the paper half of decision 029. A visit is a pin and the menu's pages; nothing is
asked of the counter and nothing is keyed at a desk.

- **Everything a traveller filters on comes off the card at review** (decision 033): the name, the
  kind of kitchen (pure veg when the card says so or prints no meat, fish or egg), and Jain only
  where the card prints Jain dishes or a Jain section. Nothing the card does not say is claimed.
- **No question is ever put to a kitchen** about Jain, vrat or onion and garlic. A dietary answer
  the card does not give stays unanswered, never a "no" (decision 029's rule on that stands).
- **Form numbers stay**, as the pin's name: they are how a menu is matched to its place in the
  collectors' app (decision 041).
- **A place need not be visited.** A restaurant's own online menu, with its location, is read the
  same way (the `Pull a QR menu for review` workflow): Creek City and Antar Al Mankhool,
  27 September. The traveller sees no difference, and nothing in the app says which were visited.
